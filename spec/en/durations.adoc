= Durations and intervals
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

A duration is a length of time, such as 30 minutes.
An interval is the span between two points in time, such as the validity of a permit.

== Durations

[[DAT-020]]
*DAT-020.* A duration MUST use the {abbr-iso-first} 8601 form, within the grammar of link:https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3339[{abbr-rfc-first} 3339], appendix A.
Examples are `PT30M` for 30 minutes and `P1Y2M` for one year and two months.
Weeks stand alone, as in `P2W`, and no unit has a decimal fraction.
The units run from the largest to the smallest with no gap, so one year and one day is `P1Y0M1D`.
The form names each unit, so a reader never has to guess whether a number counts seconds or days.

[[DAT-021]]
*DAT-021.* A duration that measures elapsed time exactly SHOULD use only hours, minutes and seconds, such as `PT36H`.
A day lasts 23 or 25 hours when the clocks change, and months have different lengths.
`P1D` therefore means "the same time on the next day", which is not always 24 hours.
Use days, months and years only for a calendar meaning, such as a notice period in days.

[[DAT-022]]
*DAT-022.* A duration SHOULD NOT be a bare number.
A bare number hides its unit, and the reader then has to look it up.
When a field must hold a count of time units, its name gives the unit, such as `timeoutSeconds`.

== Intervals

[[DAT-023]]
*DAT-023.* An interval MUST be a {abbr-json-first} object with a `start` member and an `end` member of the same type.
The start is part of the interval and the end is not.
An interval without an end leaves the `end` member out.
Intervals of this kind join end to end with no gap and no overlap.
They need no artificial end such as `23:59:59.999`.

[source,json]
----
{"start": "2026-11-04", "end": "2027-11-04"}
----

This permit is valid from 4 November 2026 up to the end of 3 November 2027.
An interface can still show the last valid day to people.

== JSON form and LinkML type

[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Type |JSON form |LinkML
|Duration |String, such as `"PT30M"` |Type `Duration`, based on `string`, with a pattern
|Interval of instants |Object with `start` and `end` |Class `InstantInterval`
|Interval of dates |Object with `start` and `end` |Class `DateInterval`
|===

LinkML has no built-in duration type, so the `Duration` type is a string with a pattern.
