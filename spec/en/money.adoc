= Money
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

An amount of money is exact, and it always has a currency.
These rules keep both facts in the data.

[[DAT-050]]
*DAT-050.* An amount of money MUST be a {abbr-json-first} object with an `amount` member and a `currency` member.
An amount without its currency is ambiguous as soon as a second currency appears.
Public bodies pay and receive foreign currencies more often than their systems expect.

[source,json]
----
{"amount": "12.50", "currency": "CAD"}
----

[[DAT-051]]
*DAT-051.* The `currency` member MUST be the alphabetic code of the {abbr-iso-first} 4217 list, in capitals, such as `CAD`.
The alphabetic code is the form that people and most systems recognize.
The numeric code, such as `124`, adds a lookup and is easy to mistake for an amount.

[[DAT-052]]
*DAT-052.* The `amount` member MUST be a string in the decimal form of the xref:numbers.adoc[number rules], never a {abbr-json} number.
A {abbr-json} number would pass through binary floating point in most parsers.
A sum of such values can then be off by a fraction of a cent.

[[DAT-053]]
*DAT-053.* A payable amount MUST have as many decimals as the minor unit of its currency in {abbr-iso} 4217.
Examples are an invoice total, a balance, a payment or a settled sum.
The Canadian dollar has two decimals and the Japanese yen has none.
Rates and unit prices can carry more decimals, and their schema states how many.
A fixed number of decimals shows that the amount is final, and it compares as written.

[[DAT-054]]
*DAT-054.* A system that rounds an amount MUST state the rounding method and the step at which it rounds.
Examples of methods are "half up" and "half to even".
Two systems that round in different ways or at different steps disagree by a cent.
An audit then flags the difference.
Where a law or a tax rule sets the method, the system follows that rule and cites it.

[[DAT-055]]
*DAT-055.* A system MUST NOT compute amounts of money with binary floating-point numbers.
Programming languages offer decimal types for this purpose.
Binary floating point turns `0.10 + 0.20` into a value that is not `0.30`.

== JSON form and LinkML type

[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Type |JSON form |LinkML
|Amount of money |Object with `amount` and `currency` |Class `Money`
|Amount |String, such as `"12.50"` |Type `DecimalString`
|Currency |String, such as `"CAD"` |Type `CurrencyCode`, based on `string`, with a pattern
|===
