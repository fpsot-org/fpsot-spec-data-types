= Dates and times
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

These rules use the date and time formats of link:https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3339[{abbr-rfc-first} 3339].
That {abbr-rfc} is a profile of {abbr-iso-first} 8601-1:2019.
It keeps one way to write each value, so programs can parse and sort the values as text.

== Instants

An instant is one moment on the global time line, such as the moment a record changed.

[[DAT-010]]
*DAT-010.* An instant MUST be an {abbr-rfc} 3339 date and time in {abbr-utc-first}, with a capital `T` and `Z`.
An example is `2026-11-04T15:30:00Z`.
One form makes instants from different systems compare and sort correctly as plain text.
An offset such as `-05:00` adds nothing to a moment.
It also hints at a time zone that it does not name.

[[DAT-011]]
*DAT-011.* A reader MUST accept instants with no fractional seconds and with one to nine digits of them.
Systems keep time at different precisions, from whole seconds to nanoseconds.
The schema of a field states the precision that its producer writes.

[[DAT-012]]
*DAT-012.* A reader MUST accept an instant with `60` seconds.
The {abbr-rfc} 3339 format allows that value for a leap second.
Most clocks spread a leap second over a longer period and never show `60`.
A rare leap second must still not make a reader reject a record.
In 2022, the General Conference on Weights and Measures chose to widen the allowed gap with the Earth's rotation.
The change comes by 2035, and leap seconds then stop.

[[DAT-013]]
*DAT-013.* Exchanged data MUST NOT hold a date or an instant as a number, such as seconds since 1970.
A reader cannot tell the unit of such a number, nor check it by eye.
Large values also exceed the integers that every {abbr-json-first} parser reads exactly.

== Dates

[[DAT-014]]
*DAT-014.* A date without a time MUST be a full date such as `2026-11-04`, never an instant at midnight.
A date of birth or a due date names a calendar day, not a moment.
Midnight in {abbr-utc} falls on the day before in Canada, so a converted instant would show the wrong day.

== Local dates and times

[[DAT-015]]
*DAT-015.* When the local clock time matters, a system MUST store the local date and time and a time zone.
Examples are a future appointment, or a deadline that a law sets in local time.
Governments change the rules of their time zones, sometimes with little notice.
An instant computed today would then point to the wrong hour on the local clock.
The local date and time has no offset, since the time zone gives it.
The time zone is a separate field, as the xref:time-zones.adoc[time zone rules] set.

[source,json]
----
{"localDateTime": "2027-03-14T09:00:00", "timeZone": "America/Toronto"}
----

== Times of day

[[DAT-016]]
*DAT-016.* A time of day without a date MUST use the 24-hour form `HH:MM:SS` of {abbr-rfc} 3339, such as `08:30:00`.
Fractional seconds can follow, as in instants.
Opening hours and recurring schedules need a time of day that belongs to no single date.
The 24-hour form has no "a.m." or "p.m." to translate or misread.

== JSON form and LinkML type

[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Type |JSON form |LinkML
|Instant |String, such as `"2026-11-04T15:30:00Z"` |Type `Instant`, based on `datetime`, with a pattern for `Z`
|Date |String, such as `"2026-11-04"` |Type `CalendarDate`, based on `date`
|Local date and time |Object with `localDateTime` and `timeZone` |Class `ZonedLocalDateTime`, with the type `LocalDateTime`
|Time of day |String, such as `"08:30:00"` |Type `TimeOfDay`, based on `string`
|===

LinkML's own `datetime` type accepts any offset, so the `Instant` type adds a pattern that requires `Z`.
In a {abbr-json} Schema, LinkML's `time` type requires an offset, so `TimeOfDay` uses a string with a pattern.
